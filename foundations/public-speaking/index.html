    Public Speaking¶

    Today's goal: plan and deliver a short talk (a demo, a team update, a meetup talk or a speech at an event) with a clear structure, a steady voice and less fear.

    Fear is normal: use it¶

    Almost everyone feels nervous before speaking to a group. Your heart beats faster to give you energy. The goal isn't to remove the nerves; it's to prepare so well that the nerves don't matter.

    • Nobody can see how nervous you feel. You look much calmer than you think.
    • The audience wants you to succeed.
    • The first 30 seconds are the hardest. Memorise your opening and you'll get through them.

    The simple talk structure¶

    1. Hook — a question, a surprising number or a short story (30 s)
    2. Point — what this talk is about, in one sentence
    3. Three parts — no more than three main ideas, each with one example
    4. Recap — repeat the three ideas in one line
    5. Close — a call to action or a memorable final sentence

    A 5-minute team talk

    Hook: "Last month, we spent 31 hours waiting for code reviews." Point: "Today I'll share three changes that cut that in half." Parts: 1. Smaller PRs · 2. A review rota · 3. Review labels (nit, blocker). Recap: "Small PRs, a rota, and labels." Close: "Let's try it for two sprints. I'll share the numbers at the next retro."

    Phrases that guide your audience¶

    Purpose Phrases
    Opening Good morning, everyone. Thank you for having me. / Let me start with a question.
    Agenda Today I'll cover three things: first… second… and finally…
    Moving on That brings me to my second point. / Now let's look at…
    Example For example… / Let me show you what I mean.
    Emphasis The most important thing is… / If you remember one thing today, it's this.
    Recap So, to sum up… / Let's quickly recap.
    Closing Thank you for listening. I'm happy to take questions.

    Voice and body¶

    Do Avoid
    Speak 20% slower than normal Rushing to "get it over with"
    Pause after important points Fillers: um, basically, actually, like
    Look at different people, one sentence each Reading from slides or your phone
    Stand with feet apart, hands relaxed at waist level Hands in pockets, crossed arms, swaying
    Vary your voice: louder for key points One flat tone for the whole talk
    Smile at the start and the end Apologising: "Sorry, I'm not a good speaker…"

    Preparing in 5 steps¶

    1. Write your three main points and one example for each.
    2. Say it out loud three times. Don't write a script to memorise word for word.
    3. Record yourself once on your phone and watch it at normal speed.
    4. Time it. Cut anything that goes over.
    5. Practise the first and last 30 seconds until they're automatic.

    Handling questions¶

    Situation Say
    Good question "Great question." Then answer in 2–3 sentences.
    You don't know "I don't know, but I'll find out and share it with everyone by tomorrow."
    Long, unclear question "Just to make sure I understand: you're asking about…?"
    Off-topic "Interesting point. Can we discuss it after the session?"
    Silence "A question I often get is…" (answer it yourself)

    Conversation: preparing with a colleague¶

    YouDo you have five minutes? I'm presenting at the all-hands tomorrow, and I'm really nervous.
    MeenaOf course. How long is your slot?
    YouFive minutes. I've got twelve slides.
    MeenaThat's too many. What are the three things you want people to remember?
    YouThat deploys are faster now, that outages are down, and that we need two more people for on-call.
    MeenaPerfect. One slide each, plus a title slide. How are you going to start?
    YouI was going to introduce myself and the team.
    MeenaStart with a number instead. Something surprising.
    You"Last year, a deploy took two hours. Today it takes eighteen minutes."
    MeenaThat's a great opening! Practise it five times tonight, and you'll be fine.
    YouThanks, Meena. That really helps.

    Practice¶

    Prepare a 60-second talk on "My favourite tool at work", using: hook → point → three reasons → close. Record it, then watch it once and note one thing to improve.

    Speak today¶

    • Give your 60-second talk out loud three times
    • Count your filler words in the recording, and try again with pauses instead
    • Volunteer for one small speaking moment this week (stand-up summary, demo, toast)
