    Polite Requests & Offers¶

    Today's goal: ask for things, offer help, ask permission and refuse, politely. The same words sound rude or kind depending on how you build the sentence.

    The politeness ladder¶

    Give me the bill. — an order; sounds rude
    Can I have the bill? — fine with friends, shops, cafés
    Could I have the bill, please? — polite: use this as your default
    Would you mind bringing the bill? — very polite
    I was wondering if you could… — very formal; for big requests and senior people

    Requests: asking someone to do something¶

    Pattern Example
    Can you + verb…? Can you send me the link?
    Could you + verb…, please? Could you speak a bit slower, please?
    Would you + verb…? Would you check this for me?
    Would you mind + verb-ing…? Would you mind closing the window?
    Do you think you could…? Do you think you could review my PR today?

    Answering "Would you mind…?": "No, not at all." = Yes, I'll do it! (because "mind" = object).

    Asking for things¶

    • Could I have a glass of water, please?
    • I'd like (= I would like) a window seat, please.
    • Can I get a medium cappuccino? (cafés)
    • Do you have this in black?

    Asking permission¶

    Pattern Example Reply
    Can I…? Can I sit here? Sure. / Go ahead.
    Could I…? Could I leave 30 minutes early today? Yes, that's fine.
    May I…? (formal) May I come in? Please do.
    Is it okay if I…? Is it okay if I join by phone? Of course.
    Do you mind if I…? Do you mind if I open the window? No, go ahead.

    Offers and suggestions¶

    • Shall I call a cab for you?
    • Would you like some tea? → Yes, please. / No, thank you.
    • Can I help you with that bag?
    • Let me help you. / Let me check that for you.
    • Why don't we take a break? / How about meeting on Monday? / Let's order lunch.

    Refusing politely¶

    • "I'm sorry, I'm afraid I can't today."
    • "Thanks for asking, but I have another commitment."
    • "I'd love to, but I'm travelling that week."
    • "Unfortunately, that's not possible. What I can do is…"

    Magic words & phrases¶

    Instead of Say
    "What?" "Sorry? / Pardon? / Sorry, could you repeat that?"
    "Wait." "Just a moment, please. / Could you give me a minute?"
    "I want…" "I'd like…"
    "Give…" "Could you pass… / Could I have…"
    "Move." "Excuse me, could I get past?"
    "Tell me." "Could you let me know…?"
    "No problem" (after thanks) "You're welcome. / My pleasure. / Happy to help."

    Conversation: asking a colleague for help¶

    YouHi Meena, do you have a minute?
    MeenaSure, what's up?
    YouI was wondering if you could help me with the Kafka configuration. I'm a bit stuck.
    MeenaOf course. Would you mind waiting until 3? I'm in a meeting till then.
    YouNot at all. Shall I send you the error log before that?
    MeenaYes, please. That would help.
    YouGreat, I'll send it now. And is it okay if I share my screen when we talk?
    MeenaAbsolutely.
    YouThanks so much, I really appreciate it.
    MeenaHappy to help!

    Practice¶

    1. Make these polite.

    1. Give me the menu. 2. Repeat that. 3. I want to change my seat. 4. Wait here.
    Answers
    1. Could I have the menu, please? 2. Sorry, could you repeat that, please? 3. I'd like to change my seat, if possible. 4. Could you wait here for a moment, please?

    2. Reply. "Would you mind turning off your phone?" (You agree.) / "Would you like some coffee?" (You don't want it.)

    Answers

    No, not at all. (and turn it off) / No, thank you. I'm fine.

    3. Refuse politely. Your colleague asks you to stay late tonight to test their feature.

    Sample answer

    I'd love to help, but I can't stay late tonight. I could test it first thing tomorrow morning. Would that work?

    Speak today¶

    • Use Could I…? or Could you…? at least 5 times
    • Replace one "What?" with "Sorry, could you repeat that?"
    • Offer help once with "Shall I…?" or "Let me…"
