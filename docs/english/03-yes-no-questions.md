# Yes/No Questions

<div class="ssp-banner" markdown>
**Today's goal:** turn any sentence into a yes/no question, and answer naturally. These are the most
common questions at counters, in shops, and at work.
</div>

## Rule 1: with am / is / are, swap the order

<div class="ssp-formula">Statement: You <em>are</em> ready. → Question: <em>Are</em> you ready?</div>

| Statement | Question |
|---|---|
| The train **is** late. | **Is** the train late? |
| You **are** free today. | **Are** you free today? |
| This seat **is** taken. | **Is** this seat taken? |
| They **were** at the meeting. | **Were** they at the meeting? |

## Rule 2: with action verbs, add do / does / did

<div class="ssp-formula"><em>Do / Does / Did</em> + subject + base verb … ?</div>

| Statement | Question | Note |
|---|---|---|
| You **like** spicy food. | **Do** you **like** spicy food? | I / you / we / they → do |
| She **works** here. | **Does** she **work** here? | he / she / it → does, verb loses -s |
| They **delivered** it. | **Did** they **deliver** it? | past → did, verb goes to base |
| The bus **goes** to MG Road. | **Does** the bus **go** to MG Road? | |

!!! warning "Most common mistake"
    ❌ *Does she works here?* ❌ *Did you went?* → ✅ **Does she work here?** ✅ **Did you go?**
    The **do/does/did** already shows the tense, so the main verb stays in its base form.

## Rule 3: with can / will / should / have (helper verbs), swap the order

| Statement | Question |
|---|---|
| I **can** pay by UPI. | **Can** I pay by UPI? |
| The shop **will** open at 10. | **Will** the shop open at 10? |
| We **should** book early. | **Should** we book early? |
| You **have** finished. | **Have** you finished? |
| There **is** a pharmacy nearby. | **Is there** a pharmacy nearby? |

## Answering yes/no questions

| Question | Short answer (natural) | Full answer (clear) |
|---|---|---|
| Is the train on time? | Yes, it is. / No, it isn't. | No, it's 20 minutes late. |
| Do you take cards? | Yes, we do. / No, we don't. | Yes, we take all cards and UPI. |
| Did you get my email? | Yes, I did. / No, I didn't. | Not yet. Could you send it again? |
| Can I sit here? | Sure! / Of course. / Sorry, it's taken. | Sorry, my friend is sitting here. |
| Have you eaten? | Yes, I have. / Not yet. | Not yet. Shall we go together? |

!!! tip "Don't answer with only \"Yes\" or \"No\""
    A bare "No." can sound rude. Add a little: **"No, sorry, we don't."** or **"Yes, of course!"**

## Polite yes/no questions you'll use every day

- **Is this** the queue for tickets?
- **Do you** have this in a larger size?
- **Does this** bus go to the railway station?
- **Can I** pay by card?
- **Is there** a lift / washroom / ATM nearby?
- **Did you** get a chance to look at my PR?
- **Have you** booked the tickets?
- **Are you** using this chair?

## Conversation: at a medical shop

```dialogue
You: Hi. Do you have something for a cold?
Pharmacist: Yes, we do. Do you have a fever too?
You: No, I don't. Just a runny nose and a sore throat.
Pharmacist: Okay. Is it for you or for a child?
You: It's for me. Can I take it with my blood-pressure tablets?
Pharmacist: Yes, that's fine. Do you have any allergies?
You: No, I don't. Is it okay to take it before food?
Pharmacist: Better after food. Take it twice a day.
You: Thank you. Can I pay by UPI?
Pharmacist: Sure. Scan the QR code here.
```

## Practice

**1. Make yes/no questions.**

1. The shop is open. 2. You live near here. 3. He finished the work. 4. We can park here. 5. There is a lift.

??? success "Answers"
    1. Is the shop open? 2. Do you live near here? 3. Did he finish the work? 4. Can we park here? 5. Is there a lift?

**2. Fix the questions.**

1. *You are coming?* 2. *Does he likes tea?* 3. *Did you saw the email?* 4. *Is available the room?*

??? success "Answers"
    1. Are you coming? 2. Does he like tea? 3. Did you see the email? 4. Is the room available?

**3. Give a short answer and a full answer.** "Do you work on weekends?" / "Is the office far from here?"

??? success "Sample answers"
    No, I don't. I only work Monday to Friday. / No, it isn't. It's about ten minutes by auto.
