## The basic English sentence

English has a fixed order. Most Indian languages put the verb at the end; English puts it **second**.

<div class="ssp-formula">Subject → <em>Verb</em> → Object → Place → Time</div>

| Subject | Verb | Object | Place | Time |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| I | drink | tea | at the office | every morning. |
| Priya | fixed | the bug | in the payment service | yesterday. |
| We | will deploy | the update | to production | on Friday. |
| My son | plays | cricket | in the park | after school. |

!!! tip "Hindi vs English order"
    Hindi: *Main roz subah chai peeta hoon* (I every morning tea drink).
    English: **I drink tea every morning.** Subject, then verb, then the rest.

## am / is / are (the verb "be")

| Subject | be | Example |
|---|---|---|
| I | am | I **am** a developer. → I'm a developer. |
| He / She / It / one person | is | She **is** at home. → She's at home. |
| You / We / They / many | are | They **are** late. → They're late. |

Use "be" for: **who/what** (I'm an engineer), **where** (He's in the meeting), **how** (We're tired),
**age** (She's 28), and **descriptions** (The app is slow).

!!! warning "Common mistake"
    ❌ *I am work in Infosys.* → ✅ **I work at Infosys.** (Don't use "am" with another action verb in
    the simple present.)

## Negative sentences

| Type | Positive | Negative |
|---|---|---|
| be | The server **is** up. | The server **isn't** (is not) up. |
| be (I) | I **am** ready. | I**'m not** ready. |
| Action verb (I/you/we/they) | I **like** coffee. | I **don't like** coffee. |
| Action verb (he/she/it) | He **works** on weekends. | He **doesn't work** on weekends. |
| Past | We **finished** the task. | We **didn't finish** the task. |
| can | I **can** come. | I **can't** come. |

<div class="ssp-formula">Subject + <em>don't / doesn't / didn't</em> + base verb</div>

After **doesn't / didn't**, the verb goes back to its base form: ❌ *He doesn't works* → ✅ **He doesn't work**.

## Make sentences longer (and clearer)

Add **details** in the order: *what → where → when → why*.

1. I called the bank.
2. I called the bank **this morning**.
3. I called the bank **this morning** **about my blocked card**.
4. I called the bank this morning about my blocked card, **because I couldn't pay at the shop**.

## Conversation: at the reception

```dialogue
Receptionist: Good morning. How can I help you?
You: Good morning. I'm here for an interview. My name is Ashish Gupta.
Receptionist: Welcome. Who are you meeting?
You: I'm meeting Ms. Rao from the engineering team. The interview is at 10:30.
Receptionist: Okay. She's in a meeting right now. Can you wait for ten minutes?
You: Sure, no problem. Is there a washroom nearby?
Receptionist: Yes, it's down the corridor, on the left.
You: Thank you. I'll wait here.
```

## Practice

**1. Put the words in order.**

1. coffee / every day / drinks / she → ______
2. in Pune / my parents / live → ______
3. the report / sent / yesterday / I / to my manager → ______

??? success "Answers"
    1. She drinks coffee every day. 2. My parents live in Pune. 3. I sent the report to my manager yesterday.

**2. Am, is or are?**

1. The meeting ___ at 3 pm. 2. We ___ ready. 3. I ___ in the office today. 4. The new laptops ___ fast.

??? success "Answers"
    1. is 2. are 3. am 4. are

**3. Make these negative.**

1. The app works offline. 2. I am free today. 3. They called me.

??? success "Answers"
    1. The app doesn't work offline. 2. I'm not free today. 3. They didn't call me.

**4. Fix the mistake.**

1. *I am stay in Bengaluru.* 2. *He don't like meetings.* 3. *Yesterday I go to office.*

??? success "Answers"
    1. I stay (or live) in Bengaluru. 2. He doesn't like meetings. 3. Yesterday I went to the office.
