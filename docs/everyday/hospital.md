!!! danger "Emergency first"
    In India, call **112** (all emergencies) or **108** (ambulance in many states). Say: **"I need an
    ambulance at [address + landmark]. A man has collapsed and isn't responding."**

## Key words

| Word / phrase | Meaning |
|---|---|
| reception / registration / OPD | front desk / signing up / outpatient department |
| emergency / casualty / ER | for urgent cases |
| ward / ICU / room | where admitted patients stay |
| admit / discharge | keep in hospital / allow to go home |
| consultant / specialist / resident doctor | types of doctors |
| blood test / X-ray / scan / report | tests and results |
| cashless / reimbursement / TPA | insurance: hospital bills the insurer / you claim later / insurance desk |
| visiting hours / attendant pass | when visitors are allowed / pass for the person staying |

## Questions you'll ask

- **Where is the registration counter / emergency?**
- **Which doctor should I see** for back pain?
- **How long is the waiting time?**
- **Is this hospital cashless** for [insurance company]?
- **When will the reports be ready?**
- **What are the visiting hours?** Can I stay overnight?
- **What is the estimated cost** of the treatment / surgery?
- **When can he be discharged?**
- **Could you explain the report** in simple words?

## Questions you'll be asked

| They ask | You answer |
|---|---|
| Patient's name and age? | Ramesh Gupta, 64. |
| What's the problem? | He has chest pain and difficulty breathing since this morning. |
| Any existing conditions? | Yes, diabetes and high blood pressure. |
| Is he on any medication? | Yes, metformin and amlodipine. |
| Any allergies? | He's allergic to penicillin. |
| Do you have insurance? | Yes, here's the card. Is it cashless here? |
| Who is the attendant? | I am, his son. |

## Conversation 1: at the emergency desk

```dialogue
You: Please help. My father has chest pain and he's sweating a lot.
Nurse: Okay, bring him here. What's his name and age?
You: Ramesh Gupta. He's 64.
Nurse: When did the pain start?
You: About forty minutes ago. It's spreading to his left arm.
Nurse: Does he have any medical conditions?
You: Diabetes and high blood pressure. He takes metformin and amlodipine.
Nurse: Any allergies?
You: Yes, penicillin.
Nurse: Okay. The doctor will see him right now. Please complete the registration at the counter.
You: Thank you. Do you need his insurance card?
Nurse: Yes, give it to the insurance desk after registration.
```

## Conversation 2: talking to the doctor and billing

```dialogue
Doctor: Your father is stable now. The ECG shows a minor blockage.
You: Thank you, doctor. What does that mean? Is it serious?
Doctor: It needs treatment, but we caught it early. We'd like to admit him for two days for tests.
You: Okay. What tests will you do?
Doctor: Blood tests and an angiogram. Then we'll decide the next step.
You: What's the estimated cost, and is it covered by insurance?
Doctor: The billing desk will give you an estimate. Most of it should be covered.
(At the billing desk)
You: Hi, I need an estimate for my father, Ramesh Gupta, admitted in ICU bed 4.
Staff: It's about ₹85,000 for two days. We've sent the cashless request to your insurer.
You: How long does the insurance approval take?
Staff: Usually four to six hours. We'll inform you.
You: Thank you. Could you give me the estimate in writing?
```

## Visiting a patient

- "**Which ward is** Mr. Ramesh Gupta in? He was admitted yesterday."
- "**How is he doing** today?"
- "**Can I bring** him home-cooked food?"
- "**Is there anything he needs?**"
- (To the patient) "**How are you feeling?** Get well soon. **Take rest**."

## When things go wrong

- "**We've been waiting for two hours.** Could you tell us how long it will be?"
- "**The bill has charges** for tests that weren't done."
- "**The insurance approval is taking too long.** Who can I speak to?"
- "**I'd like a second opinion.**"
