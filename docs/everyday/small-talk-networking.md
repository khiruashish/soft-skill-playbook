# Small Talk & Networking

<div class="ssp-banner" markdown>
**Small talk is how people start to trust each other.** Most opportunities (jobs, partnerships, mentors)
start with a 3-minute chat. Ask, listen, follow up.
</div>

## The 4-step conversation

<div class="ssp-stack">
<div>1. Open <span>— comment on the shared situation</span></div>
<div>2. Ask <span>— open questions about them</span></div>
<div>3. Connect <span>— find something in common, share a little</span></div>
<div>4. Exit <span>— gracefully, with a next step</span></div>
</div>

## Openers

=== "Conference / meetup"

    - "Hi, I'm Ashish. **What did you think of that talk?**"
    - "**What brings you here** today?"
    - "Is this your **first time** at this conference?"

=== "Office / new team"

    - "Hi, I don't think we've met. I'm Ashish, I work on the checkout team."
    - "**How was your weekend?**"
    - "Are you **working on anything exciting** this quarter?"

=== "Travel (airport, train, flight)"

    - "**Are you travelling for work** or holiday?"
    - "Have you **been to Singapore before**? Any tips?"
    - "This delay is long, isn't it? **Where are you heading?**"

=== "Waiting / lift / café"

    - "**Busy day today**, isn't it?"
    - "That smells great. **What did you order?**"

## Keep it going: the FORD topics

| Topic | Question |
|---|---|
| **F**amily / home | "Are you from Bangalore originally?" |
| **O**ccupation | "What kind of work do you do?" → "What do you enjoy most about it?" |
| **R**ecreation | "What do you do when you're not working?" |
| **D**reams | "What are you excited about this year?" |

**Follow-up magic phrases:** "Oh, how did you get into that?" · "That's interesting, tell me more." ·
"What was that like?"

## Introduce yourself (networking pitch)

<div class="ssp-say" markdown>
"I'm Ashish. I'm a backend engineer working on a grocery marketplace. Lots of Kafka and microservices.
**Lately I've been figuring out** how to make checkout reliable during flash sales. What about you?"
</div>

Keep it short and interesting, and end with a question back to them.

## Exiting politely

- "It was **great talking to you**. I'm going to grab a coffee before the next session."
- "I don't want to keep you. **Can we connect on LinkedIn?**"
- "Let me **introduce you to** Priya. She's working on exactly that."

## Follow up within 24 hours

```text
Hi Neha, great meeting you at the Kafka Summit yesterday! I really enjoyed our chat about
consumer lag. Here's the blog post I mentioned: [link]. Would love to stay in touch.
— Ashish
```

## Topics to avoid with strangers / at work

Politics · religion · salary · weight/appearance · gossip about colleagues.

## Practice conversations

### At a tech meetup

```dialogue
You: Hi, I'm Ashish. That was a great talk on Kafka, wasn't it?
Neha: Hi, I'm Neha. Yes! The part about consumer lag was really useful.
You: Are you working with Kafka at the moment?
Neha: Yes, we use it for payments at my company. What about you?
You: Same. We use it for order events in a grocery app. How long have you been working on payments?
Neha: About four years. Before that I was in banking.
You: Interesting! What made you switch?
Neha: I wanted to build products, not just maintain them.
You: That makes sense. Would you like to connect on LinkedIn? I'd love to stay in touch.
Neha: Sure, here's my QR code.
You: Great. It was really nice meeting you, Neha. Enjoy the rest of the event!
```

### In the lift at the office

```dialogue
Colleague: Morning! Busy day?
You: Morning! Yes, we have a release today. How about you?
Colleague: Same here. Lots of meetings.
You: Did you have a good weekend?
Colleague: Yes, I went to Nandi Hills. The sunrise was beautiful.
You: Nice! I've always wanted to go. Is it crowded?
Colleague: Go early, before 5, and it's fine.
You: Good tip. Have a good day!
Colleague: You too!
```
