# Resignation Conversation

<div class="ssp-banner" markdown>
**People remember how you left more than how you joined.** Make the decision first, tell your manager
before anyone else, be grateful and brief, hand over properly, and leave everyone glad they worked
with you.
</div>

## The resignation path

<div class="ssp-stack">
<div>1. Decide <span>— offer letter signed; you're sure; you know your notice period</span></div>
<div>2. Tell your manager <span>— in person or on video, never first by email</span></div>
<div>3. Put it in writing <span>— short, polite resignation email</span></div>
<div>4. Handle the counter-offer <span>— decide beforehand what you'll do</span></div>
<div>5. Serve the notice <span>— hand over, document, stay professional</span></div>
<div>6. Exit well <span>— exit interview, farewell, stay connected</span></div>
</div>

## Before you resign: checklist

- [ ] **Signed offer letter** from the new company (not just a verbal offer)
- [ ] **Notice period** and **buyout** rules checked in your contract
- [ ] Unused **leave**, pending **bonus** or **ESOP vesting dates** checked; is it worth waiting a few weeks?
- [ ] **Personal files** removed from company devices (only personal files, never company data)
- [ ] Your **reason** in one positive sentence
- [ ] Your answer to a **counter-offer** decided

## Telling your manager

=== "Asking for the meeting"

    <div class="ssp-say" markdown>
    "Hi Priya, **do you have 15 minutes today for a private conversation?** Something I'd like to
    discuss in person."
    </div>

=== "The conversation"

    <div class="ssp-say" markdown>
    "Thank you for making time. **I've decided to resign. I've accepted an offer from another
    company**, and my last working day would be 3rd February, per my notice period.<br><br>
    This wasn't an easy decision. **I've learned a lot here, especially from you**: the checkout
    migration and leading the team were big growth steps for me. **I want to make the transition as
    smooth as possible.** I'll prepare a handover plan by Friday."
    </div>

    Keep it **short, positive and final**. You're informing them, not asking permission.

=== "\"Why are you leaving?\""

    <div class="ssp-say" markdown>
    "**It's an opportunity to work on larger-scale systems and move into an architect role**, which
    is the direction I want to grow in."
    </div>

    One positive, forward-looking reason. **Don't list complaints** in this meeting, even if you
    have them. Save constructive feedback for the exit interview, and keep it gentle.

=== "\"Where are you going?\""

    <div class="ssp-say" markdown>
    "I'd prefer to share that closer to my last day, once everything is finalised. I hope you
    understand."
    </div>

    or simply tell them if you're comfortable. Both are fine.

=== "Manager reacts badly"

    <div class="ssp-say" markdown>
    "I understand this comes at a hard time, and I'm sorry about the timing. **I'm committed to a
    proper handover** and will do everything I can to reduce the impact on the team."
    </div>

    Stay calm. Don't argue or justify yourself at length.

## The resignation email

```text
Subject: Resignation — Ashish Gupta

Dear Priya,

Please accept this email as formal notice of my resignation from my position as Senior
Software Engineer at XYZ Technologies. As per my notice period, my last working day will be
3 February 2027.

Thank you for the opportunities and support over the past four years. I'm grateful for
everything I've learned here, and I'll do my best to ensure a smooth handover.

Best regards,
Ashish Gupta
```

Keep it **short, factual, and grateful**. No reasons, no complaints. This email goes into your
permanent HR record.

## Handling the counter-offer

| Question | Think about |
|---|---|
| Why did I start looking? | Will more money fix *that* (manager, growth, tech, culture)? |
| Why is the offer coming now? | Why wasn't I valued this way before I resigned? |
| How will I be seen? | Some leaders will now see you as a flight risk. |
| What about the new company? | Backing out after accepting damages your reputation there. |

=== "Declining the counter-offer"

    <div class="ssp-say" markdown>
    "Thank you, I really appreciate the offer, and it means a lot that you want me to stay.
    **I've thought carefully, and I've made my decision.** My reasons are about the role and
    growth, not just compensation."
    </div>

=== "If you genuinely want to consider it"

    <div class="ssp-say" markdown>
    "Thank you. **Can I have until tomorrow to think it over?** I want to make the right decision
    for everyone."
    </div>

    Be honest with both companies and decide quickly.

## Notice period: leaving well

- [ ] **Handover document:** systems you own, credentials process, ongoing work, known issues, contacts
- [ ] **Knowledge-transfer sessions** recorded for the team
- [ ] **Finish or cleanly hand over** in-progress tickets; don't start big new work
- [ ] **Update runbooks / READMEs** for anything only you know
- [ ] Stay **fully engaged** until your last day. People notice.
- [ ] Ask about a **notice buyout** or early release politely if the new company needs you sooner

<div class="ssp-say" markdown>
"**Would it be possible to be released on 20th January instead of 3rd February?** I've planned the
handover so it would be complete by then, and I'm happy to be available for questions afterwards."
</div>

## Exit interview

Be **honest but constructive**. Focus on systems, not people:

| ❌ Avoid | ✅ Say instead |
|---|---|
| "My manager is terrible." | "More regular feedback and clearer growth paths would help people stay." |
| "Everything here is broken." | "Investing in on-call tooling would reduce burnout." |
| "The pay is a joke." | "Compensation was below market for my role, which was a factor." |

## Farewell message

```text
Hi everyone,

Today is my last day at XYZ. It's been an amazing four years. I've learned so much from this
team, from late-night sale days to the checkout migration we pulled off together.

Thank you for the support, the code reviews, and the chai breaks. I'll miss you all!

Let's stay in touch: linkedin.com/in/ashish-example · ashish.personal@example.com

All the best,
Ashish
```

## Don'ts

!!! danger "Never"
    - Announce your resignation to the team **before your manager knows**
    - Post about it on LinkedIn **before your last day** (or before your manager agrees on timing)
    - Take **company code, documents or customer data**, even "for reference"
    - Badmouth the company or colleagues, in the office or online
    - Mentally check out during notice; it's the last thing people remember
    - Try to recruit teammates to your new company during notice

## For team leads: when someone resigns

- **Thank them** and congratulate them, even if it hurts: "I'm sad to lose you, but this is a great step."
- Ask **once** if there's anything that would change their mind, then respect the answer.
- Ask: "**What could we have done better?**" Listen; don't defend.
- Plan the **handover together** and tell the team with them, in a positive way.
- **Celebrate their exit.** Alumni can become referrals, clients, or future re-hires.
