# Appraisal Discussion

<div class="ssp-banner" markdown>
**The appraisal is where your rating turns into money: the hike, the bonus, sometimes a new title.**
Find out how the numbers were decided, respond with data rather than emotion, and leave with either an
improvement or a written plan.
</div>

!!! info "Appraisal vs performance review"
    The [Performance Review](performance-review.md) is about **feedback and rating**. The appraisal
    discussion is about the **outcome**: hike %, bonus, promotion, and what happens if you disagree.
    In many companies they happen in the same meeting; prepare for both.

## Before the discussion

<div class="ssp-stack">
<div>1. Know the system <span>— rating scale, hike bands, bonus formula, promotion cycle</span></div>
<div>2. Know your market <span>— what your role pays outside (see Salary Negotiation)</span></div>
<div>3. Know your case <span>— top 3–5 wins with numbers (your wins file)</span></div>
<div>4. Know your ask <span>— the specific hike/title you think is fair</span></div>
<div>5. Know your floor <span>— what you'll do if the outcome is below it</span></div>
</div>

### Questions to research (or ask HR) beforehand

- How are **hike percentages** linked to ratings? Is there a range per rating?
- Is there a separate **market correction** budget for people paid below band?
- How is the **variable bonus** calculated (company %, individual %)?
- When is the **promotion** cycle, and is promotion a separate hike?
- Can a rating or hike be **reviewed or appealed**? Who decides?

## Understanding your hike letter

| Item | Ask yourself |
|---|---|
| Fixed pay increase % | Is it above or below inflation (~5–6%) and the company average? |
| New CTC breakdown | Did fixed go up, or only variable/benefits? |
| Variable / bonus | Target vs actual payout %? When is it paid? |
| ESOPs / RSUs | New grant? Vesting schedule? |
| Effective date | Is it backdated (arrears)? |
| Title / level | Did your level change, or just the title? |

!!! warning "Watch the CTC trick"
    A "15% CTC hike" can mean only 8% more fixed pay if the rest is variable, insurance or
    gratuity. Always compare **monthly in-hand** and **fixed** pay year on year.

## Scripts

=== "Good outcome"

    <div class="ssp-say" markdown>
    "Thank you, I really appreciate this and the recognition. **I'd like to keep this momentum.**
    What should I focus on so I'm considered for promotion in the next cycle?"
    </div>

=== "Hike below expectation"

    <div class="ssp-say" markdown>
    "Thank you for walking me through this. I'll be honest: **the hike is lower than I expected**
    given my rating and impact. This year I led the checkout migration and cut cloud costs by
    ₹35 lakh. **Could you help me understand how the number was decided?**"
    </div>

    Then:

    <div class="ssp-say" markdown>
    "Based on market data for my role, I'm currently about 15% below the range. **Is there a
    possibility of a market correction**, either now or at the mid-year review?"
    </div>

=== "Manager says \"It's out of my hands\""

    <div class="ssp-say" markdown>
    "I understand budgets are set centrally. **Could you take my case to HR / your manager** for a
    correction? I'm happy to put the details in writing so it's easy to share. And if nothing can
    change now, **could we agree in writing on a review in 6 months** with clear criteria?"
    </div>

=== "Promised promotion didn't happen"

    <div class="ssp-say" markdown>
    "Last cycle we agreed that leading the platform migration would make the case for promotion.
    I delivered it in September. **What changed?** I'd like to understand the gap, and get a
    specific timeline and criteria in writing this time."
    </div>

=== "Rating and hike don't match"

    <div class="ssp-say" markdown>
    "My rating is 'Exceeds Expectations', but the hike is close to the company average. **Is the
    hike band for my rating different from what I understood?** I want to make sure there's no
    error before we close this."
    </div>

    Mistakes do happen in the system. Asking politely is fine.

=== "Asking for non-cash alternatives"

    <div class="ssp-say" markdown>
    "If the fixed hike can't change, **could we look at** a one-time bonus, an extra ESOP grant,
    a certification budget, or a title change? Any of these would show recognition of this year's
    work."
    </div>

## Escalating properly (if the outcome is clearly unfair)

1. **Sleep on it.** Don't react in the meeting or on Slack.
2. Ask your manager for the **reasoning in writing**.
3. Write a **calm, factual email** with your achievements and data.
4. Use the **official review / appeal process** (HR, skip-level).
5. Decide on your **floor**. If it isn't met, start planning quietly and professionally.

```text
Subject: Request to review my appraisal outcome

Hi Priya,

Thank you for our discussion today. As mentioned, I'd like to request a review of my
appraisal outcome. Summary:

• Rating: Exceeds Expectations
• Key outcomes: checkout migration (−60% failed orders), ₹35L/yr cloud savings,
  mentored 4 engineers (2 promoted)
• Market data: current fixed pay is ~15% below the range for Senior Engineer in Bangalore

I'd appreciate it if this could be considered for a correction, or a defined review at
mid-year. Happy to discuss further.

Best regards,
Ashish
```

## Phrases that help and phrases that hurt

| ❌ Avoid | ✅ Use instead |
|---|---|
| "This is unfair, everyone else got more." | "Can you help me understand how this was decided?" |
| "I'll leave if this doesn't change." | "I want to build my career here. What would it take?" |
| "My friend at X got 40%." | "Market data for my role shows a range of…" |
| Silent acceptance, then complaining later | "Can I take a day to think about this and come back to you?" |
| Discussing your hike with teammates | Keep your numbers private; it's usually company policy too. |

## For team leads delivering appraisals

- **Know the numbers before the meeting:** the band, the reasoning, what's flexible and what isn't.
- **Lead with the outcome**, then the reasoning. Don't make people wait 20 minutes for the number.
- **Don't blame "management"** for numbers you supported. If you fought for more, you can say so.
- **Allow emotion:** "I understand this is disappointing. Take your time."
- **Never promise** what you can't guarantee ("Next year for sure").
- End with a **clear growth plan** and a check-in date.

## Practice conversation: Hike below expectation

```dialogue
Manager: Your hike this year is 7%.
You: Thank you for explaining. Honestly, it's lower than I expected given my rating.
Manager: I understand. The budget was tight this year.
You: Market data shows I'm about 15% below the range for my role. Is there a possibility of a market correction?
Manager: That's not something I can approve myself.
You: Could you take it to HR? I'm happy to put the details in writing.
Manager: Yes, send me an email and I'll forward it.
You: Thank you. And if nothing changes now, could we agree on a review in six months?
Manager: That's fair. I'll note it.
```
