# Interviews

<div class="ssp-banner" markdown>
**An interview is a structured conversation between two people deciding whether to work together.**
You're interviewing them too. Prepare stories, not memorised answers.
</div>

## "Tell me about yourself" (60–90 seconds)

**Present → Past → Future**

<div class="ssp-say" markdown>
"I'm a backend engineer with 15 years of experience, currently leading a team of 6 building a
grocery marketplace on Java and Kafka **(present)**. Before that, I spent 8 years in fintech, where I
scaled a payments platform to 2 million transactions a day **(past)**. I'm looking for a role where
I can shape architecture and grow engineers at a larger scale, and that's what drew me to this team
**(future)**."
</div>

=== "Beginner version"

    <div class="ssp-say" markdown>
    "I recently graduated in Computer Science, and I interned at XYZ, where I built a REST API for
    inventory tracking in Spring Boot. I enjoy backend work and learning how systems scale, and I'm
    excited about this role because your team works with Kafka and microservices."
    </div>

## Behavioural questions: STAR

Prepare **6–8 stories** that you can adapt:

| Theme | Example question |
|---|---|
| Conflict | "Tell me about a disagreement with a teammate." |
| Failure | "Tell me about a time you failed." |
| Leadership | "When did you lead without authority?" |
| Pressure | "Describe a tight deadline you handled." |
| Impact | "What's the project you're proudest of?" |
| Learning | "When did you learn something quickly?" |
| Feedback | "Tell me about critical feedback you received." |
| Decision | "Describe a hard technical trade-off." |

!!! example "STAR answer (failure)"
    **S:** Our release went out with a data-migration bug that corrupted 2% of user addresses.
    **T:** As tech lead, I owned the fix and the follow-up.
    **A:** I rolled back within 20 minutes, wrote a repair script, personally emailed affected users,
    and added migration dry-runs to CI.
    **R:** All data restored in 4 hours; no repeat in 2 years. **What I learned:** never run
    migrations without a dry-run on a production snapshot.

## Questions to ask the interviewer

- "What does success look like in the first 90 days?"
- "What's the biggest challenge the team is facing right now?"
- "How are technical decisions made here?"
- "How do engineers grow here? What did the last promotion look like?"
- "What do you personally enjoy most about working here?"

## Handling tricky moments

| Moment | Say |
|---|---|
| You don't know the answer | "I haven't worked with that directly. Here's how I'd approach it…" |
| You need time to think | "Let me take a moment to think about that." |
| Salary question (early) | "I'd like to learn more about the role first. What's the budgeted range?" |
| "Why are you leaving?" | Stay positive: "I'm looking for bigger scale and ownership." Never criticise your current employer. |
| "Any weaknesses?" | A real one + what you're doing about it: "I used to over-explain to executives. I now practise one-sentence summaries." |

## Practice conversation: The 'Tell me about yourself' moment

```dialogue
Interviewer: Let's start. Could you tell me a little about yourself?
You: Sure. I'm a backend engineer with nine years of experience. Right now I lead a team of five building a grocery delivery platform on Java and Kafka.
Interviewer: What did you do before that?
You: I spent six years in fintech, where I helped scale a payments system to two million transactions a day.
Interviewer: And why are you interested in this role?
You: I'd like to work on bigger distributed systems and grow into an architect role. Your platform team does exactly that.
Interviewer: Great. Tell me about a hard technical decision you made recently.
```
