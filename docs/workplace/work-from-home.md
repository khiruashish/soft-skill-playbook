# Work from Home

<div class="ssp-banner" markdown>
**When you work from home, people judge you by your output and your communication. Nobody sees how
hard you're working.** Be easy to reach, visible, and reliable, and still switch off at the end of the
day.
</div>

## The remote trust formula

<div class="ssp-stack">
<div>Responsive <span>— reply within an agreed time during working hours</span></div>
<div>Visible <span>— progress shared in writing, without being asked</span></div>
<div>Reliable <span>— do what you said, when you said</span></div>
<div>Present <span>— camera on, engaged in meetings, not multitasking</span></div>
<div>Sustainable <span>— clear start and end to the day</span></div>
</div>

## Asking for work from home

=== "Occasional WFH day"

    <div class="ssp-say" markdown>
    "I'd like to **work from home on Thursday**. I have a plumber coming in the morning. I'll be
    online for the stand-up and all my meetings, and reachable on Slack all day."
    </div>

=== "Regular hybrid / remote arrangement"

    <div class="ssp-say" markdown>
    "I'd like to propose **working from home on Mondays and Fridays**. My commute is 3 hours a day,
    and I'd use that time for focused work. **I suggest we try it for 2 months**: I'll keep my core
    hours at 10 to 6, join every meeting on video, and send a short update each Friday. Then we
    review whether it's working."
    </div>

    Lead with **what the company gets**, offer a **trial period**, and suggest **how you'll
    measure** it.

=== "Temporary (health, family, relocation)"

    <div class="ssp-say" markdown>
    "My father has surgery next month, and I'll need to be in Lucknow for about 3 weeks. **Could I
    work remotely during that time?** I'll keep my usual hours and meetings. If there are a couple
    of days I need off, I'll take leave for those."
    </div>

=== "Request turned down"

    <div class="ssp-say" markdown>
    "I understand. **Could you help me understand the concern?** If it's about availability or
    collaboration, I'd be happy to try a shorter trial, or one day a week, so we can see how it goes."
    </div>

## Daily communication from home

### Start and end your day visibly

```text
🟢 Morning (team channel)
Good morning! Today: finishing slot-booking API tests, then PR review for Meena.
Available all day except 2–3 pm (doctor).

🔴 End of day
Logging off. Done: slot API tests ✅, Meena's PR reviewed.
Tomorrow: integrate with the cart. No blockers.
```

### Status & presence

| Status | Use it for |
|---|---|
| 🟢 Active | Working, reachable |
| 🎧 Focus / heads-down | "Deep work till 1 pm. Ping me if urgent." |
| 🍽️ Lunch / away | "Back at 2." |
| 📵 Off | Outside working hours |

!!! tip "The response-time agreement"
    Agree with your team: e.g. **Slack within ~1 hour, email within a day, phone call for real
    emergencies.** That frees everyone from having to watch Slack all day.

## Video calls from home

- [ ] **Camera on** for 1:1s and small meetings; at eye level, face lit from the front
- [ ] **Neutral background** or a simple blur
- [ ] **Headset with a mic** (laptop mics pick up the pressure cooker and the doorbell)
- [ ] **Mute** when not speaking; unmute *before* talking
- [ ] **Test** audio/screen share 2 minutes before important calls
- [ ] **Close** other tabs and notifications before screen sharing

### Home interruptions: what to say

| Situation | Say |
|---|---|
| Background noise | "Sorry, there's some construction noise. I'll stay on mute when I'm not speaking." |
| Family member walks in | (Smile) "Sorry, one moment." Mute, handle it, return: "Sorry about that, I'm back." |
| Internet drops | (Chat/phone) "Lost connection, rejoining in 2 minutes." After: "Sorry, what was the last point I missed?" |
| Power cut | Message the team: "Power cut here, on mobile data. Joining audio-only." |
| Need to step away | "I need to step away for 10 minutes. I'll catch up from the notes." |

## Staying visible when you're not in the office

- **Write down your work:** design docs, PR descriptions, weekly updates. In a remote team, written
  work is how people see what you do.
- **Speak in meetings.** Remote people are easy to forget; contribute in the first 5 minutes.
- **Use the chat** in meetings to add links, ideas, and "+1, agree".
- **Do virtual coffee chats:** 15 minutes, camera on, no agenda, 1–2 per week.
- **Celebrate others publicly** in team channels.
- **Turn up in person** for planning days, team events and big launches, if you're hybrid.

## Writing well for remote work

| ❌ Unclear | ✅ Clear |
|---|---|
| "Hi" … (waits) | One complete message with the full question |
| "Can we connect?" | "Can we do a 15-min call today about the slot API contract? Free 3–5 pm." |
| "Done?" | "Is the payment PR merged? I need it to start the refund work." |
| Long paragraph | Bullet points, the ask in **bold**, a deadline with a time |

## Boundaries at home

- **Fixed start and end times**, with a short "commute" ritual (walk, tea) to start and end the day
- **Separate workspace** where possible, even a corner of a room
- **Real lunch break** away from the screen
- **Notifications off** after hours (except on-call)
- **Tell your family your schedule:** "Meetings 11–1, please knock first."

## Feeling isolated?

- Pair-program on video for a few hours a week
- Join or start a **non-work channel** (cricket, cooking, pets)
- Work from a **café or co-working space** once a week
- Tell your manager. Isolation affects performance, and a good lead will help.

## For leads managing remote teams

- Judge **outcomes**, not online status.
- Hold **regular 1:1s on video**; ask "How are you, really?"
- Run meetings **remote-first**: if one person is remote, everyone joins from their laptop.
- **Write decisions down** so remote people aren't left out of hallway conversations.
- Watch for **silent struggle**: remote people often hide being blocked or burned out.
