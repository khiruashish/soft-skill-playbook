# Meetings & Presentations

<div class="ssp-banner" markdown>
**If you don't speak in meetings, people won't know what you know.** Speaking up is part of the job, and
so is running a meeting well and giving a demo people remember.

## How to speak up (when you find it hard)

<div class="grid cards" markdown>

-   :material-numeric-1-circle:{ .lg .middle } __Prepare one point__

    ---

    Read the agenda beforehand and write one question or opinion.

-   :material-numeric-2-circle:{ .lg .middle } __Speak early__

    ---

    The first 5 minutes are the easiest. Waiting makes it harder.

-   :material-numeric-3-circle:{ .lg .middle } __Build on others__

    ---

    "Adding to what Meena said…" is a low-risk way in.

-   :material-numeric-4-circle:{ .lg .middle } __Ask a question__

    ---

    A good question counts as a contribution: "How will we measure success?"

</div>

### Phrases to enter a conversation

- "Can I add something here?"
- "Building on that point…"
- "I have a slightly different view."
- "Before we move on, can I raise one risk?"
- "Sorry to interrupt. Quick clarification:"

### When someone interrupts you

<div class="ssp-say" markdown>
"Sorry, Rahul, **let me just finish this point**, then I'd love to hear yours."
</div>

## Running a meeting

- [ ] **Purpose** in the invite: "Decide on X" or "Review Y" (not "Discuss")
- [ ] **Agenda** with time boxes
- [ ] Only the **people needed** to make the decision
- [ ] Start on time; **recap the goal** in the first minute
- [ ] **Park** off-topic items: "Great point. Let's take that offline."
- [ ] End 5 minutes early with **decisions + owners + dates**
- [ ] Send **notes within an hour**

## Demos & presentations

### Structure

1. **Hook (30 s):** the problem, in the user's words. "Customers kept asking: where's my order?"
2. **Show (most of the time):** the live demo, following the happy path
3. **Impact:** numbers, or what's now possible
4. **Next steps / asks**
5. **Q&A**

### Tips

- **Rehearse twice out loud.** Once alone, once with a friend or AI.
- **Have a backup:** screenshots or a recording in case the demo breaks.
- **Increase font size** (Ctrl/Cmd +) and close Slack and email notifications.
- **One message per slide.** Slide titles should be sentences: "Search is 3× faster", not "Results".
- **Pause after key points.** Silence makes them stick.

### Handling Q&A

| Situation | Response |
|---|---|
| Good question you can answer | Answer in 1–3 sentences, then "Does that answer it?" |
| You don't know | "I don't know, but I'll find out and send it to everyone by tomorrow." |
| Hostile question | "That's a fair concern." Then answer calmly, with facts. |
| Off-topic | "Great question. Can we take that offline after this?" |
| Long rambling question | "If I understand, you're asking about X. Is that right?" |

## Video call etiquette

- Camera on for 1:1s and small meetings
- Mute when not speaking; unmute *before* you start talking
- Look at the camera, not the screen, when making your main point
- Use the chat for links, not side conversations
- Say your name before speaking in large calls: "Ashish here. Quick question."

## Practice conversation: Handling a tough question in a demo

```dialogue
Director: This looks good, but what happens if the payment gateway goes down during a sale?
You: That's a fair concern. We retry three times, and then we switch to a backup gateway automatically.
Director: How long does the switch take?
You: Under five seconds. Customers don't see an error.
Director: Have you tested it?
You: Yes, we tested it last week with fake failures. I can share the results after the meeting.
Director: Please do.
```
