# Giving & Receiving Feedback

<div class="ssp-banner" markdown>
**Today's goal:** give feedback that is specific, kind and useful, and receive feedback (even unfair
feedback) without becoming defensive.
</div>

## Why feedback is hard

- We worry about **hurting** people or **being disliked**.
- We give **vague** feedback ("good job", "needs improvement") that nobody can act on.
- When we receive it, we hear **criticism of us**, not of our work.

## Giving feedback: the SBI model

<div class="ssp-formula">Situation → <em>Behaviour</em> → Impact → (then ask or suggest)</div>

| Part | Example |
|---|---|
| **Situation** | "In yesterday's client demo…" |
| **Behaviour** (what you saw/heard, not your opinion of them) | "…you explained the new search feature using the client's own data…" |
| **Impact** | "…and the client said it was the clearest demo they've had." |
| **Next** | "Could you show the team how you prepared it?" |

### Positive feedback (do it more often)

- "**The way you** handled the angry customer on the call was really calm. **It** stopped the escalation."
- "**Thanks for** writing the runbook. **It saved me** an hour during last night's incident."

### Improvement feedback (private, timely, specific)

<div class="ssp-say" markdown>
"Can I share some feedback about this morning's stand-up? **When you** went into the database details for
ten minutes, **the meeting** ran over and two people missed their next call. **Next time**, could you
share the headline in stand-up and take the details offline?"
</div>

| ❌ Vague or personal | ✅ Specific and about behaviour |
|---|---|
| "You're careless." | "Two PRs this week were merged without tests." |
| "Great job!" | "Your test for the empty-cart case caught a real bug." |
| "You need to communicate better." | "I didn't know the release was blocked until Friday. A message on Tuesday would have helped." |
| "You always interrupt." | "In the last two meetings, you finished Priya's sentences before she was done." |

## Receiving feedback

<div class="ssp-stack">
<div>1. Pause <span>— breathe; don't reply immediately</span></div>
<div>2. Thank <span>— "Thanks for telling me."</span></div>
<div>3. Clarify <span>— "Can you give me an example?"</span></div>
<div>4. Reflect <span>— take a day if you need to</span></div>
<div>5. Act & follow up <span>— "I tried your suggestion; did you notice a difference?"</span></div>
</div>

| Instead of | Say |
|---|---|
| "Yes, but…" | "I hadn't seen it that way. Tell me more." |
| "That's not true!" | "Can you help me understand what you saw?" |
| "Everyone does that." | "Thanks. I'll work on it." |
| Silence and hurt | "I'd like to think about this and come back to you tomorrow." |

## Asking for feedback

- "**What's one thing** I could do differently in design reviews?"
- "**On a scale of 1–10**, how clear was my presentation? What would make it a 10?"
- "**What should I keep doing**, and what should I stop doing?"

## Conversation 1: giving feedback to a peer

```dialogue
You: Hi Arun, do you have five minutes? I'd like to share some feedback about the PR reviews.
Arun: Sure. Is something wrong?
You: Not wrong, just something I've noticed. On my last three PRs, the comments said things like "this is bad" without explaining why.
Arun: Oh. I was in a hurry, I guess.
You: I understand. The impact is that I spend time guessing what you mean. Could you add a short reason, or a suggestion?
Arun: That's fair. I'll do that. Anything else?
You: No, and to be clear, your reviews catch real bugs. That's really valuable.
Arun: Thanks for telling me directly.
```

## Conversation 2: receiving tough feedback

```dialogue
Manager: I want to share some feedback about the client call yesterday.
You: Sure, please go ahead.
Manager: When the client asked about the delay, you said "it's not our fault, the bank changed the API." It sounded defensive.
You: Thanks for telling me. Can I ask what you would have said?
Manager: Acknowledge their concern first, then explain the cause briefly, and focus on the new plan.
You: That makes sense. I'll try that in the next call.
Manager: Great. I know the bank really did cause it, by the way.
You: I appreciate that. Could you give me feedback after the next call too?
Manager: Absolutely.
```
