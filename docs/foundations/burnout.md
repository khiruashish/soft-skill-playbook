# Burnout & Wellbeing

<div class="ssp-banner" markdown>
**Burnout is not a sign of weakness, and it isn't solved by working harder.** It builds slowly, until
work that used to energise you starts to drain you. This page covers how to spot it, how to talk
about it, and how to recover, whether it's you or someone on your team.
</div>

!!! warning "This is a communication guide, not medical advice"
    If you feel persistently hopeless, can't sleep or function, or have thoughts of harming yourself,
    please **talk to a doctor or mental-health professional**, or reach a crisis helpline in your
    country right away. Many companies also offer a free, confidential **Employee Assistance
    Programme (EAP)**: ask HR. Reaching out early is the right move.

## What burnout looks like

Burnout is commonly described as having three parts:

<div class="ssp-stack">
<div>Exhaustion <span>— tired all the time; rest doesn't refill the tank</span></div>
<div>Cynicism <span>— "Why bother?"; distance from work, team and users</span></div>
<div>Reduced effectiveness <span>— simple tasks feel hard; you doubt your skills</span></div>
</div>

### Warning signs to watch for

| In yourself | In a teammate |
|---|---|
| Dreading Monday from Saturday | Camera off, quieter in meetings |
| Working longer but getting less done | Messages at midnight and weekends |
| Irritable over small things | Short, sharp replies; more conflict |
| Can't switch off; checking Slack in bed | Missing deadlines they used to hit easily |
| No energy for hobbies, family, exercise | Cancelling leave; "I'll rest after this release" |
| Headaches, poor sleep, getting sick often | Cynical jokes about the work or company |

### Common causes in tech

- **Workload:** permanent crunch, too many priorities, never-ending on-call
- **Lack of control:** shifting requirements, decisions made without you
- **Lack of recognition:** effort goes unnoticed
- **Unfairness:** some people carry the team, others don't
- **Values conflict:** being asked to ship things you believe are wrong
- **Isolation:** remote work without real connection

## Talking to your manager

The hardest step is often saying it out loud. You don't have to use the word "burnout" if it doesn't
feel right. Describe the **impact** and ask for **specific changes**.

=== "Raising it"

    <div class="ssp-say" markdown>
    "I want to be honest with you about something. **For the last couple of months I've been running
    on empty.** I'm working late most nights, and I'm still falling behind, and it's starting to
    affect my sleep and the quality of my work. **I'd like to figure out together how to make this
    sustainable.**"
    </div>

=== "Asking for specific changes"

    <div class="ssp-say" markdown>
    "A few things would really help: **taking me off the on-call rota for the next month**, and
    **dropping or delaying the reporting feature** so I can focus on the migration. And I'd like to
    **take a week off** after the release on the 15th."
    </div>

    Come with **2–3 concrete requests**. It's much easier for a manager to say yes to something
    specific.

=== "If you need time off"

    <div class="ssp-say" markdown>
    "I need to take some time off to recover. I've spoken to my doctor, and **I'll be on medical
    leave for two weeks** starting Monday. I'll hand over my current work to Ravi tomorrow."
    </div>

    You don't owe anyone medical details. "Health reasons" is enough.

=== "If the response is dismissive"

    <div class="ssp-say" markdown>
    "I understand there's a lot of pressure on the team. **But if nothing changes, I won't be able to
    keep doing my best work**, and I want to. Can we look at the priorities together this week?"
    </div>

    If it continues, talk to HR, your skip-level manager, or the EAP. Your health comes first.

## Recovering: what actually helps

- [ ] **Real rest:** leave without laptop or Slack, not "working holidays"
- [ ] **Sleep first:** consistent sleep and wake times; no screens in the last hour
- [ ] **Move your body:** a daily walk counts
- [ ] **Hard stop each day:** a fixed logoff time and a shutdown ritual
- [ ] **Reduce, don't just reorganise:** drop work, don't only reprioritise it
- [ ] **Reconnect:** friends, family and hobbies that have nothing to do with screens
- [ ] **Small wins:** finish small, visible tasks to rebuild a sense of progress
- [ ] **Professional support:** a therapist or counsellor; many EAPs offer free sessions

See [Leave & Boundaries](../workplace/leave-and-boundaries.md) and
[Work from Home](../workplace/work-from-home.md) for scripts to protect your time.

## Prevention: daily and weekly habits

| Habit | Why |
|---|---|
| Say no (or "not now") to new work when full | Workload is the biggest driver |
| Take all your annual leave, spread across the year | Recovery has to be regular |
| Keep one non-work commitment every week | It gives you a reason to stop |
| Keep a wins file | Counters the "nothing I do matters" feeling |
| Have a weekly 1:1 with honest check-ins | Problems get caught early |
| Rotate on-call fairly; keep it compensated | Prevents silent overload |

## For team leads: spotting & supporting

=== "Checking in"

    <div class="ssp-say" markdown>
    "I've noticed you've been online late a lot recently, and you seem a bit stretched. **How are you
    doing, honestly?** No judgement. I'd rather know early so I can help."
    </div>

    Then **listen**. Don't jump straight to fixes. Ask: "What would help most right now?"

=== "Taking action"

    - **Remove work**, not just "take care of yourself": reassign tasks, push deadlines, pause on-call
    - **Insist on leave** for people who keep postponing it, and don't contact them during it
    - **Protect them** from escalations for a while
    - **Follow up** weekly, quietly

=== "Fixing the system"

    - Track **overtime, after-hours messages and on-call load** as team health signals
    - Plan sprints at **real capacity**, around 70–80% of theoretical
    - **Push back on leadership** about unrealistic timelines (see [Talking to the CEO](../workplace/talking-to-ceo.md))
    - **Model it yourself:** take leave, log off on time, don't send midnight messages

!!! danger "Don't say"
    - "Everyone's tired, just push through this release."
    - "You should manage your time better."
    - "Let's talk about it after the launch."
    - "Just do some yoga." (on its own, without changing workload)

## A note for experienced engineers

High performers are often the most at risk: you're trusted with everything, you find it hard to say
no, and part of your identity is being the person who saves the day. **Being sustainable is a senior
skill.** A team that depends on one person working late isn't a strong team, and fixing that is part
of your job.

## Practice conversation: Telling your manager you're overloaded

```dialogue
You: Can I be honest about something? I've been working late every night for a month, and I'm exhausted.
Manager: Thank you for telling me. I hadn't realised it was that bad.
You: I'm worried it's affecting my work. I made two mistakes last week that I normally wouldn't.
Manager: What would help most right now?
You: Moving the reporting feature to next sprint, and taking me off on-call for two weeks.
Manager: I can do both. And please take a few days off after the release.
You: Thank you. That really helps.
```
