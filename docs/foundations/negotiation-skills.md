# Negotiation Skills

<div class="ssp-banner" markdown>
**Today's goal:** negotiate calmly and fairly, whether it's a deadline at work, the price at a shop, rent
with a landlord or terms with a vendor, so both sides walk away satisfied.
</div>

## Negotiation is a conversation, not a fight

Good negotiators **prepare**, **listen more than they talk**, and look for a deal that works for **both**
sides. You'll use the same skills later in [Salary Negotiation](../career/salary-negotiation.md) and with
clients.

<div class="ssp-stack">
<div>1. Prepare <span>— your goal, your limit (walk-away point), your alternatives</span></div>
<div>2. Ask & listen <span>— find out what the other side really needs</span></div>
<div>3. Propose <span>— make a clear first offer with a reason</span></div>
<div>4. Trade <span>— give something only when you get something</span></div>
<div>5. Agree & confirm <span>— summarise, then put it in writing</span></div>
</div>

## Key ideas

| Idea | What it means | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Walk-away point** | The worst deal you'll accept | "I won't pay more than ₹30,000 rent." |
| **BATNA** (best alternative) | What you'll do if there's no deal | "If not this flat, the one in HSR is ₹31,000." |
| **Anchor** | The first number sets the range | Make the first offer when you know the market |
| **Interests vs positions** | *Why* they want something matters more than *what* they say | The PM says "Friday" because of a client demo, so maybe only the demo part is needed by Friday |
| **Trade, don't concede** | Never give something for nothing | "If I take on this extra feature, which one can move to next sprint?" |

## Phrases for every stage

| Stage | Phrases |
|---|---|
| Exploring | What's most important to you here? / What's driving the deadline? / What flexibility do you have? |
| Proposing | Based on [reason], I'd suggest… / Would you consider…? / What if we…? |
| Trading | If you can…, then I can… / I could agree to that, provided that… |
| Holding firm | I understand, but that doesn't work for me because… / That's below what I can accept. |
| Pausing | Let me think about it and get back to you tomorrow. |
| Closing | So, to confirm, we've agreed… / Could you put that in writing? |

!!! tip "Silence is powerful"
    After you make an offer, **stop talking**. Let the other person respond. Nervous people fill the
    silence and lower their own offer.

## Conversation 1: negotiating a deadline at work

```dialogue
PM: We need the export feature by Friday for the client demo.
You: I want to help with the demo. What exactly will the client see on Friday?
PM: They want to see a report downloaded as Excel.
You: Okay. The full feature, with PDF, scheduling and email, needs two weeks.
PM: Two weeks is too late.
You: What if we deliver Excel export only by Friday, for the demo, and the rest by the 20th?
PM: Can you include PDF too?
You: If I add PDF, I'll need to move the search bug fix to next week. Which matters more?
PM: The demo matters more. Excel and PDF by Friday, the bug fix next week.
You: Great. So, to confirm: Excel and PDF export by Friday, scheduling and email by the 20th, and the search fix next week. I'll put this in the ticket.
```

## Conversation 2: negotiating with a vendor

```dialogue
Vendor: The annual maintenance contract for your office ACs is ₹1,20,000.
You: Thanks. We have 20 units. Another vendor quoted ₹95,000 for the same service.
Vendor: Our response time is faster: four hours instead of twenty-four.
You: That's valuable. Could you match ₹1,00,000 if we sign for two years?
Vendor: For two years, I can do ₹1,05,000.
You: If you include two free gas refills each year, we can agree on ₹1,05,000.
Vendor: Okay, two free gas refills per year. Deal.
You: Great. Please send the contract with the four-hour response time and the refills written in.
```

## Everyday negotiations

- **Market:** "That's a bit high. Could you do ₹400 if I take two?"
- **Landlord:** "Other flats nearby ask for three months' deposit. Would you accept three?"
- **Car dealer:** "If I book today, can you include the accessories kit and free first service?"
- **Team:** "I'll take the on-call on Diwali week if you cover my New Year week."

## Mistakes to avoid

- ❌ Accepting the first offer immediately
- ❌ Giving a reason the other side can easily reject ("I can't afford it")
- ❌ Getting angry or making threats you won't carry out
- ❌ Forgetting to confirm the deal in writing
