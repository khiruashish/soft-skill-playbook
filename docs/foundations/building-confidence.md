# Building Confidence

<div class="ssp-banner" markdown>
**Confidence doesn't come before action. It comes from repeating the action.** You don't need to feel
confident to *act* confident. The feeling follows the practice.
</div>

## Why you feel nervous (and why it's normal)

- **Fear of judgement:** "They'll think I don't know enough." Most people are thinking about themselves,
  not about you.
- **Language anxiety:** speaking in a second language makes everyone feel slower. Clear, simple English
  beats fancy English every time.
- **Impostor syndrome:** common among *experienced* developers too. A 15-year engineer may know the code
  well and still feel lost in a room of executives.
- **Lack of preparation:** this is the most common cause, and the easiest one to fix.

## The confidence loop

<div class="ssp-stack">
<div>1. Prepare <span>— write 3 bullet points before any important conversation</span></div>
<div>2. Act <span>— speak up once, even briefly</span></div>
<div>3. Survive <span>— notice that nothing terrible happened</span></div>
<div>4. Reflect <span>— one thing that went well, one to improve</span></div>
<div>5. Repeat <span>— each loop makes the next one easier</span></div>
</div>

## 10 practical habits

1. **Speak in the first 5 minutes of every meeting.** Even "Good morning, everyone" or "I agree with
   Rahul's point" counts. The longer you wait, the harder it gets.
2. **Prepare 3 bullet points** before any 1:1, demo, or difficult conversation.
3. **Slow down by 20%.** Nervous people speak fast. A slow pace sounds calm and senior.
4. **Pause instead of filling.** Swap "umm" for a 1-second pause. Pauses sound thoughtful.
5. **Stop over-apologising.** Keep "sorry" for real mistakes. Swap "Sorry for the delay" for
   "**Thanks for your patience.**"
6. **Keep a wins file.** Write down every bug you fixed, compliment you got, problem you solved. Read it
   before reviews and interviews.
7. **Say "I don't know — I'll find out by X."** It's a confident sentence.
8. **Record yourself** explaining a concept for 2 minutes. Watch it once. You'll fix 3 habits immediately.
9. **Volunteer for small visibility:** a 5-minute demo, a stand-up summary, a lunch-and-learn.
10. **Talk to strangers in low-stakes places:** a café, a train, an airport. It trains the same muscle
    you use at work. See [Everyday Life](../everyday/index.md).

## Body language and voice

| Signal | Low confidence | High confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Posture | Hunched, arms crossed | Upright, shoulders relaxed, open hands |
| Eyes | Looking down or at the screen | Natural eye contact (3–5 s, then look away) |
| Voice | Rising tone at the end ("It's done?") | Falling tone at the end ("It's done.") |
| Pace | Rushed, no pauses | Steady, with pauses before key points |
| Hands | Fidgeting, touching face | Still, or gesturing at waist level |
| Video calls | Camera off, mic muted until called | Camera on, eyes on lens when making a point |

!!! tip "The 2-minute reset (before a big meeting)"
    1. Stand up. Roll your shoulders back.
    2. Breathe **in for 4, hold for 4, out for 6**, three times.
    3. Say your first sentence out loud once.
    4. Remind yourself: *"I'm here because I know something useful."*

## Reframes for negative self-talk

| Thought | Reframe |
|---|---|
| "My English isn't good enough." | "Clear and simple is enough. People care about the idea." |
| "They'll find out I don't know everything." | "Nobody knows everything. Asking good questions is a senior skill." |
| "I made a mistake in that meeting." | "I noticed it, so I can fix it next time. That's growth." |
| "I'm too junior to speak to the CEO." | "The CEO wants information from people closest to the work. That's me." |
| "I've been doing this 15 years, I should be better at this." | "Communication is a separate skill. I can learn it just like I learned a new framework." |

## Confidence for experienced engineers

Seniors have a different trap. They know so much that they over-explain, get defensive about legacy
decisions, or go quiet among executives. Try:

- **Be brief on purpose.** Your expertise shows in what you choose to leave out.
- **Say "I was wrong" in public.** Admitting a mistake makes you look more credible, not less.
- **Sponsor, don't just mentor:** speak juniors' names in rooms they're not in.
- **Ask for feedback on your communication**, not just your code.
