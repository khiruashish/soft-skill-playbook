    Performance Review¶

    Your rating is mostly decided before the meeting. It depends on what you did all year and how well your manager knows about it. Track your wins, write a strong self-review, and treat the conversation as planning for next year, not a verdict.

    The review cycle¶

    1. All year — keep a wins file; align on goals; ask for feedback every quarter
    2. Self-assessment — write it with impact and numbers
    3. Peer feedback — choose reviewers wisely; write good reviews for others
    4. Review meeting — listen, clarify, discuss, agree on goals
    5. After — follow-up in writing; act on feedback; check in at 3 months

    All year: the wins file¶

    A simple note you update every Friday (5 minutes):

    ## Nov 2026
- Shipped coupon validation → stopped ~₹40k/week leakage (PR #212)
- Led incident response for checkout outage; wrote post-mortem; 2 fixes shipped
- Mentored Kavya on Kafka; she now owns the consumer service
- Praise: "Clearest incident updates I've seen" — VP Eng, Slack, 12 Nov

    No surprises, in either direction

    Ask your manager every quarter: "If reviews were today, where would I land, and what would move me up?" That way, the review meeting doesn't surprise you.

    Writing your self-assessment¶

    Use Action → Impact → Evidence for every point:

    ❌ Weak ✅ Strong
    "Worked on the checkout service." "Re-architected checkout to use the outbox pattern, cutting failed orders by 60% (dashboard link)."
    "Helped juniors." "Mentored 3 engineers with weekly sessions; 2 now independently own services."
    "Fixed many bugs." "Closed 48 bugs, including 5 P1s; reduced support tickets by 30%."
    "Good team player." "Unblocked the payments team twice by building a shared test harness, saving ~2 weeks."

    Self-assessment template¶

    ## Key achievements (3–5)
1. [Action] → [Impact with number] → [Evidence/link]

## Goals from last cycle
| Goal | Result | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Reduce p95 latency < 500 ms | 420 ms | ✅ Met |
| Lead one design review | Led 3 | ✅ Exceeded |
| Get AWS certification | Postponed (release crunch) | ⚠️ Moved to Q1 |

## Growth & learning
- What I learned, what I'd do differently

## Areas to improve
- One or two honest areas + what I'm already doing about them

## Goals for next cycle
- 2–4 specific, measurable goals, ideally tied to team goals

## Support I need
- e.g. more exposure to architecture decisions; conference budget

    Common self-review mistakes

    Listing tasks instead of impact · being too modest ("I just helped a bit") · being vague about misses · forgetting work from early in the year (that's what the wins file is for) · writing 10 pages. Keep it to 1–2.

    Peer reviews¶

    Be specific, balanced and useful:

    Strengths: Meena's incident leadership during the October outage was outstanding.
Clear updates every 20 minutes kept leadership calm, and the post-mortem led to
real fixes.

To grow: In design reviews she sometimes moves to a solution before the
problem is fully discussed. Pausing to ask "what else could we do?" would bring
in more ideas from the team.
    • People who saw your best work closely
    • At least one person from another team (shows cross-team impact)
    • Ask them nicely, with context: "Would you be open to reviewing me? It'd help if you could mention the search migration we did together."

    The review meeting¶

    Mindset¶

    Go in calm and curious. Your goal is to understand the feedback and agree on a plan, not to win an argument.

    Scripts¶

    "Thanks for taking the time. I'd like to hear your feedback first, and then I'd love to talk about goals and what the next level looks like for me."

    "Thank you, that means a lot. What would it take to get to the next level over the next cycle? I'd like to make sure I'm working on the right things."

    Even with a great rating, ask for one thing to improve.

    "You mentioned I need to 'show more ownership.' Could you give me an example where I could have done that differently? I want to make sure I understand what it looks like."

    "I appreciate the feedback. I see some of it differently, and I'd like to share why. I led the checkout migration end to end, which was one of the team's top goals, and it reduced failures by 60%. Could you help me understand how that was weighed?"

    Then, if it still feels unfair:

    "I'd like some time to reflect on this. Could we follow up later this week? And what's the process if I'd like the rating reconsidered?"

    Don't

    Raise your voice · compare yourself to named colleagues ("But Rahul got…") · threaten to quit · cry foul in team channels. Stay factual and calm, and put things in writing.

    1. Pause and breathe. You don't need to respond instantly.
    2. "Thank you for being direct. This is hard to hear, but I want to understand."
    3. "Can you share specific examples?"
    4. "What would 'good' look like in the next 3 months?"
    5. "Can we check in monthly on this?"

    "I'd like to be considered for promotion to Staff Engineer. What gaps do you see between my work and that level? Could we write down 2–3 concrete goals and a timeline, and check progress quarterly?"

    "How does this rating translate into my compensation? Given my impact this year and the market for my role, I'd like to discuss whether there's room for an adjustment."

    See Salary Negotiation for full scripts.

    Questions to ask in every review¶

    • "What's the one thing that would most increase my impact next year?"
    • "What do you see as my biggest strength, and am I using it enough?"
    • "How do others (peers, stakeholders) see my work?"
    • "What does the next level look like in practice?"
    • "How can you support me in getting there?"

    After the review¶

    • Send a short summary email within 24 hours (feedback, goals, agreed support)
    • Turn feedback into 2–3 goals with measurable outcomes
    • Put a 3-month check-in in the calendar
    • Start next year's wins file today
    Subject: Summary of our performance review

Hi Priya, thanks for the review today. To summarise:
• Strengths: incident leadership, mentoring
• Focus areas: earlier risk communication, more design-doc writing
• Goals: lead 2 design docs; flag schedule risks within 2 days
• Next step: check-in on 15 Feb; promotion discussion in the next cycle
Let me know if I've missed anything.

    When you're the reviewer (team leads & managers)¶

    • Prepare

      Gather evidence across the whole year, not just the last month. Read their self-review first.

    • No surprises

      Nothing in the review should be news. Hard feedback belongs in regular 1:1s.

    • Specific & fair

      Use SBI (Situation-Behaviour-Impact). Judge outcomes and behaviour, not personality.

    • Forward-looking

      Spend half the time on growth and next-cycle goals.

    "Your rating this cycle is Meets Expectations. Strength: your code quality and reliability work, especially the retry framework. To grow to the next level: I'd like to see you drive decisions in design reviews, not just contribute. Let's set one goal around that and check in every month. What support would help?"

    Practice conversation: Asking about a rating you disagree with¶

    ManagerYour rating this year is "Meets Expectations".
    YouThank you. I'll be honest, I expected a higher rating. Could you help me understand how it was decided?
    ManagerYour delivery was strong, but we expected more leadership in design reviews.
    YouI see. I did lead the checkout migration, which was one of the team's top goals. How was that weighed?
    ManagerIt was a big factor. That's why the rating isn't lower.
    YouOkay. What would "Exceeds" look like next year, specifically?
    ManagerLeading two major designs and mentoring one engineer.
    YouThat's clear. Could we write those goals down and review them every quarter?
    ManagerAbsolutely.
