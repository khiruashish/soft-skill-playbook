    Handling Layoffs¶

    A layoff is a business decision. It is not a judgement of your worth. Many good engineers, at every level, have been laid off. This page covers what to say and ask on the day, how to talk about it with family and future employers, how to support colleagues, and how leads can deliver the news humanely.

    Your rights vary

    Severance, notice and benefits depend on your country, state, contract and company policy. Use this page as a communication guide, and get advice from HR or an employment lawyer about your specific situation before you sign anything.

    If you are laid off¶

    On the day: the meeting¶

    Layoff meetings are often short (10–15 minutes), with your manager and/or HR. You don't need to argue or decide anything in the room.

    1. Breathe — it's okay to be shocked; you can take a moment
    2. Listen — take notes, or ask for everything in writing
    3. Ask key questions — see the list below
    4. Don't sign immediately — ask how long you have to review
    5. Leave with dignity — people remember this moment

    "I understand. This is a lot to take in. Could you send me all the details in writing? I'd like to review the agreement properly before signing. How many days do I have?"

    Questions to ask HR¶

    Area Questions
    Last day & pay What's my last working day? Will I get notice pay, or garden leave? When is the final settlement paid?
    Severance How is the severance calculated? Is it negotiable? Is it tied to signing a release?
    Leave & bonus Will unused leave be paid out? Any pro-rated bonus?
    Equity What happens to unvested ESOPs/RSUs? How long do I have to exercise vested options?
    Insurance How long does health insurance continue? Can I convert it to a personal policy?
    Documents When will I get the relieving letter, experience letter and payslips?
    Reason How will this be described to future employers (layoff / role eliminated)? Will you provide a reference?
    Visa (if applicable) How does this affect my work visa? What is the grace period?
    Support Is there outplacement support, career coaching, or counselling?
    Equipment How and when do I return the laptop? Can I save personal files?

    Things to save (personal items only)

    Your payslips, offer letter, appraisal letters, and personal contacts. Write down your achievements while you still remember them. Never take company code, documents or customer data.

    Negotiating the package (politely)¶

    "Thank you for the offer. Given my 6 years here and the time it may take to find a similar role, would it be possible to extend the severance by one month, or extend health insurance until March? I'd appreciate it if you could check."

    It doesn't always work, but asking politely, once, is reasonable.

    Telling people¶

    "I have some difficult news: my company did layoffs today, and my role was affected. It's not about my performance. Around 200 people were let go. We have [X months] of savings and a severance package, and I'm going to start looking right away. I'll need your support."

    Be honest, share the plan, and let them help.

    Hi everyone,
As some of you know, my role was affected by today's layoffs. I'm grateful for the 6 years
here and especially for this team: the sale-day war rooms, the code reviews, the chai.
I'm now open to Senior Backend / Tech Lead roles. Referrals or intros would mean a lot.
Stay in touch: linkedin.com/in/ashish-example · ashish.personal@example.com

    After 6 great years at XYZ, my role was impacted by this week's layoffs.

I'm proud of what we built: the checkout platform that handled 10x sale-day traffic, and
a team culture I'll miss.

I'm now looking for Senior Backend Engineer / Tech Lead roles (Java, Kafka, distributed
systems) in Bangalore or remote. If you know of an opportunity, I'd be grateful for a
referral or introduction.

#OpenToWork

    Keep it positive, specific and forward-looking. Never criticise the company.

    Talking about it in interviews¶

    "My role was part of a company-wide layoff that affected about 15% of engineering, after the company changed strategy. I'm proud of my time there: I led the checkout re-architecture. I'm now looking for a team where I can [your goal], which is why this role interests me."

    • Say it once, briefly and calmly. There's nothing to be ashamed of, and interviewers know layoffs are common.
    • Move quickly to what you achieved and what you're looking for.
    • If asked about a gap: "I took a few weeks to rest and upskill on Kubernetes, then started interviewing."

    Looking after yourself¶

    • Take a few days to process before job-hunting hard.
    • Keep a routine: fixed hours for applications, learning, exercise, and rest.
    • Reach out: former managers and colleagues are your strongest source of referrals.
    • Set small daily goals: 3 applications, 1 networking message, 1 hour of practice.
    • Talk to someone (friends, family, a counsellor) if it's affecting your sleep or mood.
    • Remember: this is a chapter, not your whole story.

    If you survive a layoff¶

    Staying can be hard too: guilt, fear, more work.

    • Reach out to colleagues who left: offer references, introductions, and a message of support.
    • Ask your manager directly:

    "This has been a hard week for everyone. Can you share what this means for our team's priorities, and for the work that's been left behind? I want to focus on the right things."

    • Be honest about workload: "With 2 fewer people, we can't deliver all of this. What should we drop?"
    • Look after your own career: update your CV and wins file, without panic.

    For managers delivering layoff news¶

    This is one of the hardest conversations in management. Do it with humanity.

    Before:

    • Know all the facts: package, dates, benefits, the HR contact
    • Do it in person or on video, privately, never by email or in a group call first
    • Do it early in the week and day, not on a Friday evening
    • Have HR present, or immediately available
    • Plan who tells the remaining team and when

    The conversation:

    "Arun, thanks for joining. I have difficult news, and I want to be direct with you. Your role is being eliminated as part of the company-wide layoffs. Your last working day is the 30th. This decision was about budgets and strategy. It is not about your performance. I'm sorry. I know this is hard to hear. Sunita from HR will walk you through the package. And I will personally give you a strong reference and help with introductions."

    ✅ Do ❌ Don't
    Get to the point within the first minute Small talk before the news
    Say clearly that it's a business decision (if true) Say "I know how you feel"
    Allow silence and emotion Argue, defend, or debate the decision
    Offer concrete help (references, intros) Make promises you can't keep
    Follow up a day or two later Disappear after the meeting

    Telling the remaining team:

    "As you've heard, Arun and Priya were affected by today's layoffs. They were valued members of this team, and this wasn't about their work. I know this is unsettling. Here's what I know about our team's future, and here's what I don't know yet. My door is open, and we'll re-plan our priorities together tomorrow."

    Practice conversation: Questions to ask HR¶

    HRAs we discussed, your role is affected by the restructuring. Your last working day is the 30th.
    YouI understand. This is a lot to take in. Could you send me all the details in writing?
    HRYes, you'll receive an email today.
    YouHow is the severance calculated, and how long do I have to review the agreement?
    HRIt's two months' salary plus one week per year of service. You have seven days to sign.
    YouWhat happens to my health insurance and my unvested stock?
    HRInsurance continues for three months. Unvested stock is cancelled.
    YouWill you provide a reference letter, and how will this be described to future employers?
    HRYes. It will say your role was eliminated due to restructuring.
    YouThank you. I'll review everything and get back to you.
