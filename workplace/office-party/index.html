    Office Party & Team Events¶

    An office party is still work, just more relaxed. People remember how you made them feel, not what you said about Kafka. Turn up, mix with people, be warm, and leave on a good note.

    The party game plan¶

    1. Arrive — within the first 30 minutes, when groups are still forming
    2. Greet — your manager / host first, then your team
    3. Mix — talk to 3 people outside your usual circle
    4. Enjoy — join the games, food, photos; don't hide behind your phone
    5. Exit — thank the organisers and leave while you're still fresh

    Openers that always work¶

    • "Hi, I'm Ashish from the checkout team. I don't think we've properly met!"
    • "How long have you been with the company?"
    • "Which team are you on? What are you working on these days?"
    • "Hi Ma'am / Sir, I'm Ashish, I work on the delivery-tracking feature. Great event, thanks for organising it."
    • "What's been the highlight of the year for you?"
    • "Is there anything you're excited about for next year?"

    Tip

    Keep it short (2–3 minutes). Leaders want to meet many people. Don't pitch projects or ask for a promotion at a party.

    • "The food's great. Have you tried the biryani?"
    • "Are you joining the quiz? We need one more for our team."
    • "Who organised this? They did a great job."
    • "Any plans for the holidays?"
    • "Have you watched anything good lately?"
    • "Where are you from originally?"
    • "Do you do anything fun on weekends? Sports, music?"

    Joining and leaving groups¶

    Situation Say / do
    Join a group Stand at the edge, smile, wait for a pause: "Mind if I join you?"
    Bring someone in "Priya, have you met Arjun? He's the one who built the new search."
    Leave a conversation "Great chatting! I'm going to grab some food. Catch you later."
    Leave the party "Thanks so much for organising, I had a great time. See you Monday!"

    Toasts, speeches & awards¶

    "Can I have everyone's attention for one minute? This year we launched 3 cities and survived two sale days without downtime, and that's because of everyone in this room. Special thanks to the platform team for the late nights. To an even better next year. Cheers!"

    Under 60 seconds · one specific achievement · thank people by name · end with a cheer.

    "Thank you, I'm honestly surprised! This was a team effort. Meena, Ravi and Kavya did the heavy lifting on the migration. Thank you all."

    "I wasn't expecting this! I'll just say: I've learned more this year than in the last three, thanks to this team. Thank you!"

    Etiquette: do's and don'ts¶

    ✅ Do ❌ Don't
    Dress one step smarter than the dress code Drink too much; know your limit (or stick to mocktails)
    Put your phone away Talk about work problems, salaries or appraisals
    Talk to new joiners and quiet people Gossip or complain about colleagues or managers
    Take part in games, even if you're bad at them Stay only with your own team all evening
    Thank the organisers Make jokes about religion, politics, appearance
    Respect people who don't drink or eat certain food Pressure anyone to drink, dance or "loosen up"

    The next-morning test

    Before you say or post something, ask: "Would I be comfortable if my manager and HR heard this on Monday?" Party photos and videos travel fast.

    For introverts¶

    • Set a goal: 3 meaningful conversations, then you're allowed to leave.
    • Volunteer to help organise. It gives you a role and natural reasons to talk.
    • Arrive early. Small groups are easier than a full room.
    • Stand near the food or drinks. People come to you.
    • Ask questions and let others talk. Good listeners are always welcome.

    For seniors & managers¶

    • Go round every table, especially to people who don't normally see you.
    • Thank people publicly for specific work.
    • Make sure new joiners are included.
    • Leave a little early so the team can relax, and settle any team bill quietly.
