    Difficult Conversations¶

    The conversation you're avoiding is usually the one you most need to have. Stay calm, be specific, focus on the issue and not the person, and look for what both of you want.

    The 5-step model¶

    1. Prepare — facts, your goal, their likely view
    2. Open safely — "I'd like to talk about X. I want us both to come out of this better."
    3. Share facts, then feelings — "When X happened, I felt Y"
    4. Listen & understand — "Help me understand your side."
    5. Agree on next steps — specific, written, with owners

    Scripts¶

    "I can't take this on this sprint without dropping the payments work. Which should come first?"

    Other ways to say no:

    • "Not right now, but I can look at it on Monday."
    • "I'm not the best person for this. Priya knows that system better."
    • "I can do part of it: the API, but not the UI."

    "I respect your experience here, so I want to share a different view. I'm worried that skipping integration tests will push bugs into production during the sale. Could we keep tests for the checkout path only? It adds half a day."

    "Can we talk about the last few code reviews? I've noticed my PRs sit for 3–4 days, and some comments felt quite harsh. I might be misreading it. How do you see it? I'd like us to work better together."

    1. Breathe. Don't reply instantly.
    2. Thank them: "Thanks for telling me directly."
    3. Clarify: "Can you give me an example?"
    4. Own what's true: "You're right, I missed that."
    5. Commit: "Next time I'll…"

    Avoid

    "Yes, but…", "That's not fair", or bringing up their mistakes. Even if they're partly wrong, understand first, respond later (you can follow up the next day).

    (Privately) "In the demo, the caching work was presented as yours. I built most of it. I'd appreciate it if we credit each other accurately going forward. Can we agree on that?"

    (Publicly, at the next chance) "As I mentioned when I built the cache layer…"

    "I understand you're frustrated. This outage hurt your customers. Here's what we know, what we're doing, and when you'll hear from me next."

    Acknowledge the emotion → give facts → commit to the next update. Don't argue about blame.

    Phrases that calm things down¶

    Instead of Say
    "You're wrong." "I see it differently."
    "You always…" "In the last two sprints, I noticed…"
    "That's not my job." "I can't own that, but let me connect you with someone who can."
    "Calm down." "I can see this matters a lot. Let's figure it out."
    "Whatever, fine." "I'm not fully convinced, but I'll commit to it. Let's review in 2 weeks."

    Practice conversation: Disagreeing with a senior colleague¶

    SeniorLet's skip the integration tests this sprint. We don't have time.
    YouI see why. Can I share a concern?
    SeniorGo ahead.
    YouThe sale is next week. Without integration tests on checkout, we could push a bug that costs us orders.
    SeniorBut the tests take two days.
    YouWhat if we only test the checkout path? That takes half a day.
    SeniorOkay, that's a fair compromise. Let's do checkout only.
    YouThanks. I'll set it up today.
