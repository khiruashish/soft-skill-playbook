    Talking to Your Manager¶

    Your manager's three wishes: no surprises, clear status, and problems that arrive with options. Give them those three things and they'll trust you.

    What your manager actually needs¶

    They need So you should
    Predictability Share status before they ask
    Early warning Raise risks the moment you see them, not on deadline day
    Options, not just problems Bring 2 possible solutions with every problem
    Something to tell their boss Summarise in 1–2 sentences they can forward
    To know you're growing Talk about goals and ask for feedback

    The 1:1 meeting¶

    A simple agenda (send it the day before)¶

    1:1 agenda — [Date]
1. Wins this week: shipped X, fixed Y
2. In progress / risks: Z is at risk because…
3. Need from you: decision on A / intro to team B
4. Growth: feedback on my design review?

    The 1:1 belongs to you

    Don't let it turn into another status meeting. Keep 10 minutes for growth, feedback, and career.

    Scripts for common situations¶

    "Quick update on the payments API: on track for Friday. Endpoints are done, tests are 80% done. One risk: we're still waiting on the bank sandbox credentials. If they don't arrive by Wednesday, we slip a day. I've followed up with their team."

    "I've been stuck on the Kafka consumer lag for about 4 hours. I've tried increasing partitions and tuning the batch size. Could you suggest someone who's worked on this, or spare 15 minutes?"

    The 2-hour rule (beginners)

    Struggle alone for up to ~2 hours, then ask, with what you tried. Asking too late costs more than asking too early.

    "I want to flag a risk early. The search feature may slip by about 1 week. The OpenSearch mapping needs a reindex we didn't plan for. Two options:
    A) Ship on time without fuzzy search, add it next sprint.
    B) Keep full scope, ship next Friday.
    I'd recommend A. Which would you prefer?"

    "I want to hit this date, so let me be realistic about it. With the current scope, my estimate is 3 weeks, not 2. To make 2 weeks, we could drop the export feature, or add one more developer for the UI. What matters most for this release?"

    "I made a mistake: my migration yesterday dropped an index, and that's why the reports were slow. I've already restored it and reports are back to normal. To prevent it happening again, I'm adding a migration check to CI. Sorry for the trouble."

    "I'm working on my communication. What's one thing I could do differently in design reviews?"

    "I'd like to talk about my growth. Over the last year I led the checkout migration, cut deploy time by 80%, and mentored two new joiners. I believe I'm working at the senior level, and I'd like to be considered for promotion this cycle. What gaps do you see, and what would make the case clear?"

    Tip

    Bring your wins file with numbers. Ask for the gaps in writing. Agree on a timeline.

    "Can I share a concern before we commit? Building our own auth will take 6–8 weeks and gives us security risk. Keycloak gets us 90% of it in a week. If you still prefer custom, I'll commit to it fully — I just want to make sure we've weighed this."

    This is disagree and commit: voice it once, clearly, then support the decision.

    Mistakes to avoid¶

    • ❌ Hiding bad news until the deadline
    • ❌ "It's almost done" for 3 weeks running (give percentages or remaining tasks instead)
    • ❌ Complaining about teammates without a suggestion
    • ❌ Treating every 1:1 as a status report
    • ❌ Waiting for your manager to bring up your career

    Beginner vs experienced¶

    • Over-communicate status. Silence makes people worry.
    • Write down what your manager asks so you don't have to ask twice.
    • Before a 1:1, prepare: 1 win, 1 blocker, 1 question.
    • Manage up: learn your manager's goals and pressures, and help them succeed.
    • Bring proposals, not just problems: "I'd like to own the reliability roadmap."
    • Give your manager feedback too: "It would help me if decisions came with the 'why'."

    Practice conversation: Raising a risk early¶

    YouDo you have five minutes? I want to flag a risk early.
    ManagerSure, go ahead.
    YouThe search feature may slip by a week. We need a full reindex that we didn't plan for.
    ManagerHmm. What are our options?
    YouOption A: we release on time without fuzzy search and add it next sprint. Option B: we release the full feature a week later. I'd recommend A.
    ManagerI agree with A. Can you update the product team?
    YouYes, I'll message them today and copy you.
