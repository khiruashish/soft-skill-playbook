    Talking to Juniors¶

    Your job isn't to be the smartest person in the room. It's to make the room smarter. How you treat juniors decides whether they come to you with problems or hide them from you.

    Principles¶

    1. Psychological safety first. Juniors who are scared to ask hide bugs.
    2. Explain the why, not just the what. "Use a transaction here because two writes must succeed together."
    3. Ask before telling. "What do you think is happening?" builds thinking. Giving the answer builds dependence.
    4. Praise in public, correct in private.
    5. Be specific. "Good job" teaches nothing. "Your test for the null-cart case caught a real bug, nice" does.

    Teaching: the "I do, we do, you do" model¶

    I do — you demo while explaining your thinking out loud
    We do — pair programming; they drive, you guide
    You do — they do it alone; you review afterward

    Scripts¶

    "Welcome! For the first two weeks, nobody expects you to ship big features. Your only job is to learn and ask questions. There are no stupid ones here. Let's do 15 minutes every day at 4 pm for whatever came up."

    Use labels so they know how serious each comment is:

    nit:        (optional, style) "nit: could rename `d` to `deliveryDate` for clarity."
suggestion: (better approach)  "suggestion: a Map lookup would make this O(1) instead of O(n)."
question:   (curious, not a demand) "question: what happens if the list is empty?"
blocker:    (must fix) "blocker: this SQL is built by string concat — injection risk. Use a parameter."
praise:     "praise: really clean separation of the domain logic here 👏"

    Use SBI: Situation → Behaviour → Impact → (then ask)

    "In yesterday's release (situation), the PR was merged without tests (behaviour). A bug reached production and took 2 hours to roll back (impact). What got in the way of adding tests? How can I help next time?"

    "I'd like you to own the coupon-validation endpoint. Goal: reject expired and over-used coupons. Done means: endpoint + unit tests + docs. Deadline: Thursday. Check-in: let's sync Tuesday. You decide the design; I'm here if you get stuck."

    "Hey, how's the migration going? Getting stuck is completely normal on this one. It took me two days the first time. Want to walk me through where you are?"

    "This happens to everyone. I once took down staging for a whole afternoon. Let's fix it together first, then talk about how to prevent it."

    "I like that you're thinking about performance. Right now, the bottleneck is the database, not the JSON parsing, so a rewrite wouldn't help much. Can you profile it and show me where the time goes? If the data says otherwise, let's do it."

    Mistakes to avoid¶

    • ❌ "This is easy, just do X" (it isn't easy for them)
    • ❌ Taking the keyboard and fixing it yourself
    • ❌ Sarcasm in code reviews
    • ❌ Only giving feedback when something goes wrong
    • ❌ Answering in jargon without checking they understand

    Questions to ask juniors regularly¶

    • "What's one thing you learned this week?"
    • "What's confusing about our codebase that nobody explains?"
    • "What would you like to work on next?"
    • "How can I be a better mentor for you?"

    Practice conversation: Feedback on a junior's PR¶

    YouHi Kavya, I've reviewed your PR. Have you got ten minutes?
    KavyaYes. Is it bad?
    YouNot at all. The logic is clean and easy to read. There are two things to fix before we merge.
    KavyaOkay.
    YouFirst, there are no tests for the empty-cart case. What do you think happens if the cart is empty?
    KavyaOh… it might throw an error.
    YouExactly. And second, the SQL is built with string concatenation. Can you use a parameter instead?
    KavyaYes, I'll fix both today.
    YouGreat. Ping me when it's ready, and I'll approve it quickly.
